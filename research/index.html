Research

Working notes from the research behind the collection.

These are notes, not essays. They have not been through the checks an essay gets: quotations still need re-reading on the page they came from, some claims rest on a single source, and each file says which. The files are plain Markdown.

20 November 2026: rejected ideas on the standards mailing lists

A reading of five mailing-list archives for ideas that were proposed and refused, or specified and then abandoned. About fifty were confirmed in the archives. The saved copies of the pages are not kept here; each note gives the address of every message it used.

  • Notes: www-talk, 1991 to 1996 (the first style sheets, exec: URLs, client-side includes, 402 Payment Required)
  • Notes: www-style and www-html, 1995 to 2012 (the parent selector, behaviour in CSS, variables, JavaScript Style Sheets, XHTML 2.0)
  • Notes: the HTTP working group and www-dom (the 1996 cull of HTTP methods, HTTP-NG, PEP, getElementsByClassName, DOM Load and Save)
  • Notes: the WHATWG list, 2004 to 2014 (repetition templates, ping, the Ogg requirement, <main>, <device>, Web SQL)
  • Notes: es-discuss, 2006 to 2018 (ECMAScript 4, decimal, Object.observe, contains, version opt-in, paren-free syntax)

18 November 2026: untold web history story candidates

A hunt for under-reported stories in web and internet history. It vetted 31 candidates and ranked 16.

  • The report: the web's best untold fights, ranked
  • Notes: patents versus the web (ViolaWWW and the Eolas patent, and the 2001 W3C patent policy fight)
  • Notes: infrastructure power struggles (the DNS root in 1998, DigiNotar, robots.txt)
  • Notes: browser makers' hidden fights (Apple and KHTML, the EU browser choice screen, Spyglass)
  • Notes: companies and communities (ArsDigita, CDDB and Gracenote, Peacefire, Hotline)
  • Notes: the open hunt (twelve further candidates)
